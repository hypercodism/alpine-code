# Directions

## What the reference apps actually do

They look different from each other. What they share is that each choice follows from what the product is for.

**토스** (product principles, simplicity conference, TDS)
- "One Thing": each screen carries one core message; the rest is cut or moved to the next screen.
- "Clear Action": the next step is obvious at a glance, without reading. One blue full-width button at the bottom.
- "Value First": show what the person gets before asking for anything (the amount, the benefit), then the action.
- "Easy to Answer": three easy questions beat one hard one; a form is one question per screen, with a suggested
  answer.
- "Casual Concept": everyday words instead of financial terms; the copy carries as much of the design as the layout.
- Look: white and pale grey (`#f9fafb`) grounds, near-black ink (`#191f28`), one blue (`#3182f6`) for actions only,
  big bold numbers, generous radius (16 px), almost no borders or shadows; groups are separated by grey bands.

**당근** (SEED design system)
- A neighbour, not a store: warm off-white, one carrot orange (`#ff6f0f`) kept for the brand and the main action,
  everything else calm grey.
- The feed is the product: a plain list of photo-left rows (square thumbnail, title, place · time, price in bold),
  dense enough to scan, no cards.
- Posting is two taps from anywhere (a floating 글쓰기 button); words are friendly and local ("동네", "이웃").

**Apple** (Human Interface Guidelines)
- Clarity, deference, depth: the content leads and the interface steps back; chrome is translucent, thin or gone.
- Hierarchy by size and weight in one family (a 34 pt large title, 17 pt body), not by colour or boxes.
- Consistent margins and alignment; generous space; targets at least 44 pt; content respects the safe areas.
- Motion explains where things came from and went; it is short and never decorative.

**Linear** (redesign and refresh notes)
- "Don't compete for attention you haven't earned": navigation is dimmed so the work area leads.
- Themes come from three values (base, accent, contrast); the accent is a small share so the interface stays neutral.
- Borders were removed or softened until structure is "felt more than seen"; icons fewer and smaller, no coloured
  icon backgrounds.
- Dense but calm: compact rows, aligned labels and icons, Inter Display for titles and Inter for text.

**배민**
- A loud concept, held completely: B급, kitsch, humour, retro. Its own lettering (한나, 주아, 도현) comes from
  hand-painted shop signs; the copy is funny on purpose ("세상은 넓고 맛집은 많다").
- Proof that coherence is not minimalism. A playful direction is right when the concept is playful, and then the
  type, words and pictures all play the same game.

**What a 카드사 app does instead**: banners, badges, carousels, gradients and ten entry points on the home screen,
each added by a different team. There is no sentence the home screen says. Avoid that, whatever the style.

## Different directions for the look question

Two directions are different when a person could tell them apart in black and white. Vary at least the layout idea
and the type; then palette and imagery follow. For a bakery, for example:

1. **동네 빵집 (당근 참고)**: warm list, photo-left rows, Pretendard, cream and orange, friendly words.
2. **베이커리 매거진 (Kinfolk 참고)**: one big photo per screen, serif titles, lots of white, quiet brown accent.
3. **오늘의 빵 (토스 참고)**: today's count and pickup time in big numbers, one button, almost no imagery.

Three colourways of the same list are one direction, not three.
